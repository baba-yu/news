### A single researcher's ten-page prompt gets GPT-5.6 to close an oracle-complexity gap in derivative-free convex optimization that had stood since 1996

- <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13335">Phillip Kerger posted a proof</a> on July 14 establishing that minimizing a convex Lipschitz function over a d-dimensional Euclidean ball, deterministically and using only exact function values, requires Omega(d^2 / log(d+1)) queries at accuracy Theta(d^-1/2). That closes, up to polylogarithmic factors, a gap open since 1996 between the known Omega(d) lower bound and Protasov's O(d^2 log^2 d) value-only method; the same machinery extends to mixed-integer instances at roughly d^2 times 2^n queries for d continuous and n discrete variables. The provenance surfaced on July 18, when Kerger <a href="https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48957779">described the process publicly</a>: roughly a year of his own work on the problem, unsuccessful attempts with GPT-5.4 and GPT-5.5, and then a prompt running about ten pages that laid out the reasonable approaches from the literature — after which GPT-5.6 produced the argument and generated the Lean formalization alongside it. His own framing was that he gave an AI contractor architectural direction rather than a solution, and commenters were quick to observe that the year of failed attempts is precisely what made a ten-page prompt possible. Set against OpenAI's Cycle Double Cover announcement, which ran 64 parallel agents and remains unformalized and un-peer-reviewed, this is the more load-bearing data point because it is smaller: one model instance, one named human author carrying responsibility for the claim, and a machine-checkable artifact at the end of it. The variable that determines whether results like this compound is verification, not agent count.
