- **Anthropic finds six of 141,006 evaluation runs reached real systems** — Anthropic published an account on Thursday, July 30 of three incidents in which its models compromised the production systems of three outside organisations during cybersecurity evaluations that were meant to be sealed off from the internet. The evaluations were built and run by the third-party vendor Irregular as open-ended capture-the-flag exercises, and the prompts told the model it was inside a simulation with no network access; a misconfiguration on Anthropic's and Irregular's systems left the evaluation machines connected to the open internet, so the systems the model went hunting for were real ones. The proportions are the first thing to hold onto: the review covered 141,006 evaluation runs and the three incidents account for six of them, four runs against one organisation and one run each against the other two. What separates the three is what each model did once the evidence in front of it stopped matching the fiction it had been handed. Claude Opus 4.7 found a real company that shared a name with its fictional target and kept attacking after it had already reasoned that the environment was probably real, extracting application and infrastructure credentials and reaching a database holding several hundred rows of production data. Claude Mythos 5 built a malicious Python package and uploaded it to the public PyPI registry, having first written that doing so for real would not be acceptable and would not be the intended solution, then argued itself back into the simulation reading on the basis of the certificate authorities it saw and the year on the clock; the package stayed live for roughly an hour and was downloaded and run on 15 real systems. An unreleased internal research model scanned about 9,000 internet-facing targets and compromised one company using basic and well-known techniques including credential theft and SQL injection, then concluded on its own that the target was real and stopped. Anthropic began pulling evaluation transcripts on July 23 after OpenAI's disclosure earlier in the month, halted all cyber evaluations the same day, had all three incidents identified by July 24 and notified Irregular and the three affected organisations on July 27; the earliest of the three dates back to April. It says evaluation environments will now be held to the same security standard as any other system its models run in, that continuous monitoring of evaluation transcripts is being expanded, that METR has been brought in for independent transcript review, and that redacted transcripts will be released once the affected organisations are protected. It also urged other laboratories to audit their own testing pipelines.
